# Rice (Dhan/Chawal) — Complete Farming Guide for Indian Farmers

## Overview
Rice is India's most important staple food crop, cultivated across 44 million hectares. Major rice-growing states: West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, and Odisha. India is the world's second-largest rice producer after China.

## Varieties Recommended by ICAR
- **Pusa Basmati 1121**: Premium aromatic long-grain, 140-145 days, high export value.
- **Pusa 44**: High yield (80-90 q/ha) but water-intensive. Punjab/Haryana.
- **PR 126**: Short duration (123 days), saves 1 irrigation vs Pusa 44.
- **Swarna (MTU 7029)**: Most popular in eastern India, medium duration.
- **IR 64**: Medium-grain, suitable for irrigated conditions across India.
- **Sahbhagi Dhan**: Drought-tolerant, suitable for rainfed areas.
- **CR Dhan 801**: Submergence-tolerant, suitable for flood-prone areas.

## Sowing Season
- **Kharif (Main season)**: Nursery: May-June, Transplanting: June-July
- **Rabi/Boro**: November-February (eastern India)
- **Direct Seeded Rice (DSR)**: June first fortnight (saves water, labour)

## Seed Rate
- Transplanted: 20-25 kg/hectare (nursery)
- Direct seeded: 25-30 kg/hectare
- System of Rice Intensification (SRI): 5-7 kg/hectare

## Water Management
Rice requires 1200-1500mm of water during its growing season.
- **Puddled transplanting**: Maintain 5cm standing water for first 2 weeks
- **Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)**: Save 20-30% water
  - Flood field to 5cm → let water level drop to 15cm below soil → re-flood
  - Do NOT dry during flowering stage
- **SRI method**: Keep soil moist but not flooded — saves 40% water
- Stop irrigation 15 days before harvest

## Fertilizer Recommendation (per hectare)
- Nitrogen: 120-150 kg (3 split doses: basal, tillering, panicle initiation)
- Phosphorus: 60 kg (all at transplanting)
- Potassium: 60 kg (half at transplanting, half at panicle initiation)
- Zinc Sulphate: 25 kg/ha at transplanting (essential for rice)
- Use Neem-coated urea to reduce nitrogen losses

## Major Diseases
1. **Blast** (Magnaporthe oryzae): Diamond-shaped lesions on leaves, neck rot. Spray Tricyclazole 75WP (0.6g/litre) or Isoprothiolane 40EC (1.5ml/litre).
2. **Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB)**: Water-soaked lesions turning yellow. No effective chemical — use resistant varieties, avoid excess nitrogen.
3. **Sheath Blight**: Oval greenish-grey lesions on leaf sheath. Spray Hexaconazole 5EC (2ml/litre) or Validamycin 3L (2ml/litre).
4. **Brown Spot**: Brown oval spots on leaves. Improve nutrition (potash and zinc). Spray Mancozeb 75WP (2.5g/litre).
5. **False Smut**: Green/black balls on individual grains. Spray Propiconazole at boot leaf stage.

## Major Pests
1. **Stem Borer**: Dead hearts in vegetative stage, white ears at reproductive. Apply Cartap Hydrochloride 4G (25 kg/ha) or spray Chlorantraniliprole 18.5SC (0.3ml/litre).
2. **Brown Planthopper (BPH)**: Hopper burn — patches of dried plants. Spray directed at base: Pymetrozine 50WG (0.3g/litre). Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides.
3. **Leaf Folder**: Folded leaves with scraping. Spray Flubendiamide 39.35SC (0.2ml/litre).
4. **Gall Midge**: Silver shoots (onion leaf-like). Use resistant varieties, early transplanting.

## Harvesting
- Harvest when 80% grains turn golden yellow (moisture ~22-24%)
- Combine harvester or manual cutting
- Thresh within 24 hours of cutting to avoid grain loss
- Dry to 14% moisture before storage
- Use hermetic (airtight) bags for storage to prevent insects

## MSP 2024-25
- Common Rice (paddy): Rs. 2,300/quintal
- Grade A Rice: Rs. 2,320/quintal

## Stubble Management
- DO NOT burn stubble — it is illegal and harmful
- Use Happy Seeder for direct wheat sowing into rice stubble
- In-situ incorporation with rotavator
- Collect and use for composting, mushroom cultivation, or biomass power
