# Potato (Aloo), Onion (Pyaz), Tomato (Tamatar) — Complete Guide

## Potato (Aloo)

### Overview
India is the 2nd largest potato producer globally. Major states: Uttar Pradesh (35% of production), West Bengal, Bihar, Gujarat. Total production: ~55 million tonnes.

### Varieties
- **Kufri Jyoti**: Most popular, medium-late, suitable for plains and hills
- **Kufri Bahar**: Early maturing, good for UP and Bihar
- **Kufri Pukhraj**: High yielding, early maturing, golden skin
- **Kufri Sindhuri**: Red-skinned, good storage quality
- **Kufri Chandramukhi**: Early, white-skinned, heat tolerant

### Season & Sowing
- **Early crop**: September 25 - October 15
- **Main crop**: October 15 - November 15
- **Late crop**: November 15 - January (lower yields)
- Seed rate: 25-30 quintals/hectare
- Cut seed tubers to 30-40g pieces, each with 2-3 eyes
- Treat seed with Mancozeb (0.25%) for 20 minutes before planting

### Fertilizer (per hectare)
- FYM: 20 tonnes (apply 1 month before planting)
- N: 150-180 kg, P: 80 kg, K: 100 kg
- Apply full P and K + half N at planting, remaining N at earthing up
- Sulphur: 20 kg/ha for better quality

### Irrigation
- Total 8-10 irrigations required
- First irrigation: 3-5 days after planting (light)
- Critical stages: Stolon formation (30-35 days) and tuber bulking (45-60 days)
- Stop irrigation 10-15 days before harvest
- Drip irrigation saves 40% water

### Diseases
1. **Late Blight** (most devastating): Dark brown lesions on leaves, white fungal growth underneath. Spray Mancozeb 75WP (2.5g/L) preventively; Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (2.5g/L) curatively. Spray every 7-10 days in humid weather.
2. **Early Blight**: Brown spots with concentric rings on older leaves. Spray Chlorothalonil (2g/L).
3. **Black Scurf**: Black masses on tubers. Treat seed with Boric acid (3%) dip for 30 minutes.

### Storage
- Store at 2-4°C and 90-95% humidity for table potato
- For processing: 8-10°C
- Cold storage charges: Rs. 120-160/quintal/month
- Sprout suppressant: CIPC treatment before cold storage

### MSP/Market
- No MSP for potato (market-driven)
- Typical farm gate price: Rs. 500-1500/quintal
- Best marketing: Contract farming with chips companies (PepsiCo, ITC)
- APMC mandi or direct retail through FPOs

## Onion (Pyaz)

### Overview
India is 2nd largest onion producer. Major states: Maharashtra (30%), Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Rajasthan. Onion prices are highly volatile and politically sensitive.

### Varieties
- **Pusa Red**: Kharif, dark red, 120-130 days
- **Agrifound Dark Red**: Best for export, deep red colour
- **N-53 (Nasik Red)**: Most popular in Maharashtra, Rabi season
- **Bhima Shakti**: High yield, late Kharif suitable
- **Arka Kalyan**: Pink-red, stores well

### Season
- **Kharif**: Nursery June-July, transplanting August-September, harvest November-December
- **Late Kharif**: Nursery August-September, transplanting October-November, harvest February-March
- **Rabi (main season)**: Nursery October-November, transplanting December-January, harvest April-May

### Fertilizer (per hectare)
- FYM: 20-25 tonnes
- N: 100-120 kg, P: 50 kg, K: 60 kg
- Apply N in 3 splits: basal, 30 days, 45 days after transplanting
- Sulphur: 40 kg/ha (improves pungency and storage)

### Storage
- Cure onions in shade for 10-15 days after harvest
- Store in ventilated structures (not cold storage)
- Bottom storage/Kanda Chawl: Store in 1.5m raised bamboo platforms with good ventilation
- Storage losses: 25-30% normal; reduce to 10-15% with proper curing and ventilation
- Maleic Hydrazide (MH) spray 15 days before harvest reduces sprouting in storage

### Price Volatility Management
- Avoid panic selling during glut (October-November)
- Use NHRDF portal (nhrdf.org) for price information
- Participate in Price Stabilization Fund (PSF) scheme
- Consider contract farming for price assurance

## Tomato (Tamatar)

### Overview
India produces ~20 million tonnes of tomato annually. Major states: Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, Telangana, West Bengal. Price can swing from Rs. 200 to Rs. 10,000/quintal within months.

### Varieties
- **Pusa Ruby**: Determinate, early, good for fresh market
- **Arka Rakshak**: Bacterial wilt + leaf curl resistant, high yield (75-80 tonnes/ha)
- **Arka Samrat**: Triple disease resistant, excellent for Kharif
- **Pant T-3**: Suitable for processing
- **Hybrid: Abhilash, Arka Ananya**: Very high yield (100+ tonnes/ha)

### Season
- **Rabi (main)**: Nursery September-October, transplanting October-November
- **Kharif**: Nursery June-July, transplanting July-August (more disease pressure)
- **Summer**: Nursery January-February, transplanting March (needs irrigation)

### Key Practices
- Stake/trellis for indeterminate varieties — doubles marketable yield
- Mulching with black plastic or straw — reduces weeds, conserves moisture, prevents soil splash diseases
- Pruning: Remove suckers up to first flower cluster for better fruit size
- Spacing: 60cm x 45cm (single row) or 90cm x 30cm (double row on beds)

### Critical Disease: Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV)
- Transmitted by whitefly (Bemisia tabaci)
- Symptoms: Upward curling of leaves, stunted growth, no fruiting
- NO CURE once infected — prevention only:
  1. Use resistant varieties (Arka Rakshak, Arka Samrat)
  2. Yellow sticky traps (12-15 per acre)
  3. Nursery under nylon net (40 mesh)
  4. Spray Imidacloprid 17.8SL (0.3ml/L) for whitefly control
  5. Remove and burn infected plants immediately

### Post-Harvest
- Harvest at mature green or breaker stage for long-distance transport
- Ripening with ethylene gas: 100 ppm for 24-48 hours at 20-25°C
- Cold chain: Store at 12-15°C (NOT below 10°C — causes chilling injury)
- Processing options: Tomato paste, ketchup, puree, dried tomatoes
